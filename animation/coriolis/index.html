The Physics of Free Flight in Rotating Artificial Gravity

1. The External Perspective (Inertial Frame)

To an external observer floating in microgravity outside the station, a person standing on the rim is moving sideways at tangential speed $v_{\text{rim}} = \omega R$.

When they jump, no forces act on them once airborne (ignoring air resistance). They move in a straight line at constant velocity across the chord of the cylinder until the rotating floor meets their feet again:

$$\vec{r}(t) = \vec{r}_0 + \vec{v}_0 t = \vec{r}_0 + (\vec{v}_{\text{rim}} + \vec{v}_{\text{rel}})\,t$$

2. The Internal Perspective (Rotating Frame & Coriolis Effect)

To an inhabitant standing inside the cylinder, "down" is radially outward due to centrifugal acceleration $\vec{a}_{\text{cent}} = \omega^2 \vec{r}$.

When jumping "up" toward the hub:

  • The jumper carries their rim linear speed ($v_{\text{rim}}$) throughout the jump.
  • At smaller radii ($r < R$), the habitat structure needs less tangential speed ($\omega r$) to complete a rotation.
  • Because the jumper's tangential velocity exceeds the rotational speed at smaller radii, they move ahead of the floor in the spinward (forward) direction.
  • In the rotating reference frame, this deflection appears as the Coriolis acceleration:
$$\vec{a}_{\text{coriolis}} = -2(\vec{\omega} \times \vec{v}_{\text{rel}})$$

3. Benchmark: 1 km Diameter Station at 1.0g

For a $1\text{ km}$ diameter habitat ($R = 500\text{ m}$) spinning at $1.34\text{ RPM}$ to provide $1.0\text{g}$ ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$) artificial gravity at the floor:

  • A vertical jump of $v_0 = 3.0\text{ m/s}$ reaches an apex height of $h \approx 0.46\text{ m}$ ($1.5\text{ ft}$).
  • The flight duration is $t \approx 0.61\text{ seconds}$.
  • The jumper lands approximately $0.052\text{ meters}$ ($5.2\text{ cm}$ / $2.0\text{ inches}$) spinward of their launch point.

In smaller centrifuges (e.g. $R = 10\text{ m}$), this Coriolis deflection is prominent—a $3\text{ m/s}$ jump deflects the landing by over $1.3\text{ meters}$, creating strong disorientation.
