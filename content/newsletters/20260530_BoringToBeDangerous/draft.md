Dear Reader,

I started learning to play guitar again.  I got the bug one night on a camping trip with the boys.  A guy at a campsite over was noodling on his guitar at the campfire with his family, and it inspired me.

Remembering the advice of my guitarist cousin Bob, "If you can pick with your fingers nobody will ever call you a horseshit guitarist," I picked up a cheap acoustic and started to learn Travis picking.

Then Stopped.

Then started again.

I used to play as a kid, and has a little band called "Noise Shits" with my friends.  But I never practiced enough to get good.  Eventually I joined the LaRouche movement and decided heavy metal is not really music, because it's not classical.  Needing money, I took my beloved, heavily modified electric guitar to a pawn shop, and sold it for $50.  It hurt to sell it, but I convinced myself I was sacrificing my childish dreams for the greater good.

But now I want back in, so I can have fun with my kids at the campfire.  And I know that I can get at least good enough to sing some traditional songs if I just practice a little bit every day.

## Seinfeld and the Chain

In an interview a few years ago, comedian Jerry Seinfeld said something that has stuck with me.  He was describing his routine where he spends an hour a day filling up yellow legal pads with joke and other creative ideas.  That's work for him.

Same routine, day in, day out.  Trying to keep [adding links to that daily chain](https://distractedfortune.com/put-another-link-on-the-chain/).

"You have to be boring in your personal life to be dangerous in public."

An effective person spends each day being utterly predictable, building up a volume of work.  500 words a day.  30 minutes exercise per day.  20 minutes reading every day.  The neighbors can set their clock by your super boring, regular schedule.

But that boring routine builds up a massive muscle memory.  Like a trained Shaolin monk, when the moment comes, you can wield your mastery with deadly effectiveness, because you no longer need to think about how to do the parts.  Your mind becomes free to see the world before you, while your body acts as the perfect tool for the moment.

For example, take memorizing poems.  Super boring, I know.  When faced with a poem, usually it takes a few reads to have any inkling what the thing is even about.  When you read it enough times to memorize it, all of a sudden, it's an object in your mind.  You no longer need to spend the time to find out "what did it say there again?"  You can compare the beginning to the end, turn it backwards, upside down, compare it to other things you've heard.  It's yours now, so your creativity can act on the whole thing.

All it takes is a few minutes, every day, and you can build up any real skill.  You can't build a fortress in an afternoon, but you can lay one brick perfectly every morning until the walls are impenetrable.

So, I'm almost 50.  Why can't I play the guitar yet?

## Blocked

What stops a person from mastery, if it only takes a little time every day?

When I think about taking that 15 minutes to practice, suddenly my mind gets filled with noble responsibilities.

Clean the dishes.  Clean the basement.  Cut the grass.  Go to the store and buy some cheese for the pizza.  Fold the laundry.  Call the contractor.  Respond to an email.

Service some other person, by sacrificing myself.

In other words, my childhood traumas kick in.  In order to deserve love, I need to sacrifice my own time and pursuit of excellence, to help someone else achieve their goals.

By the time I get through that flood, I'm usually outside mowing the lawn.  Not practicing the guitar.

We find a million reasons to *not* work on our own self development each day, even though 15 minutes is a drop in the bucket of a day.  Personally, I can stand and look in the pantry for a whole 15 minutes no problem.  But work on a real goal?  That's hard.

## Boring Through the Soul

I wrote a few weeks ago about "[Exposure Therapy](https://distractedfortune.com/what-is-and-how-to-use-exposure-therapy/)".  In short, exposure therapy is where you identify a fear, then confront that fear in low-stakes situations to show your inner beast that it won't kill you.

For me, at least, reaching back to high school to continue the path of learning such a mediocre instrument as the guitar is incredibly heavy exposure therapy.

I am working to change my identity in to that of a powerful, successful man.  For fifteen minutes every day, I will act like a man who respects his own mission.  That fifteen minutes will look on the outside like a guy playing simple tunes on a guitar, poorly.  But on the inside, it will look like a structural battle to change the course of my life.

So tomorrow, the grass can grow a little longer, the dishes can wait in the sink.  For fifteen minutes, I will pick up the guitar and hit that C-major chord.

Thanks for reading - now get back to work.

Peter
