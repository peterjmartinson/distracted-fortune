Dear Reader,

We're trying a new experiment with homework at the Martinson household, one that might just work.

The genesis came when I was calculating the sheer volume of academic regression that happens over a three-month vacation. Left to their own devices, it's just TV, baseball, and ice cream—and the older one is already showing signs that certain subjects require a fight to stick.

My first thought was, we need to cover all the big subject that will get rusty.  Not history - the boys love that, and it sticks like the gum they leave everywhere.  But Math, Reading, and languages.  Reading's easy; I can just get them to read a certain number of pages per week of some book, any book.  Math is a little more challenging, but all we really need is some way to generate a bunch of problems for the boys.  French and Latin are tougher.  Maybe we give them translation assignments, get some French baseball news from Montreal or something.

So far so good.  The problem is, I lose track of assignments during the regular school year.  When I'm responsible for designing, assigning, and getting the boys to do the homework, there's little chance of success.

I realized the real bottleneck isn't having the assignments available (the teachers will supply some of that), it's pacing the work and having deadlines.

So, I came up with a solution right out of project management - the [Kanban Board](https://kanbantool.com/kanban-guide/kanban-history).  If you work in an office, you've walked into a meeting room with a bunch of Post-It notes stuck on the whiteboard.  You draw three columns on the board:  To Do, Doing, Done.  You write tasks on the sticky notes, one per note, and pile them all under the To Do column.  Once someone starts working on a task, that note gets moved to Doing.  When it's done, it gets moved to Done.

Instead of waiting for the Summer, I figured it's best to get them in the swing of things while schoolwork is a plenty.  So I took the boys to Staples a few days ago, and had them pick their favorite color sticky note (blue for the older, green for the younger son).  We took them home, and I wrote one current school assignment on each note - Class, Assignment, Due Date.  

For the board itself, I used the back of the door to the toy closet.  That way it's not an eyesore (just close the door), and every time the boys go for a toy, they're confronted with their backlogs.  The traditional four-panel design offers a perfect Kanban setup too.  I put a yellow sticky with "Do" on the left panel, one with "Doing" on the center column, and one with "Done" on the right panel.

The day after putting all the stickies up, Older Boy grabbed the "study for French quiz" sticky, moved it to "Doing", and did some studying.  Finally, he moved it to "Done", and I quizzed him.

Then we crumpled the note and trashed it.  Done!

Unbeknownst to the family, I also created a parallel [Trello](https://www.trello.com) board with Do, Doing, Done lists.  The cards are color-coded with green and blue for each boy, and each has a due date alarm set.  Each morning, I do a quick check to make sure the door and Trello are in sync.

Because here is the ultimate plot twist of this experiment: The real target of this system isn't the boys. It's me. They’ll do the work if prompted, but I needed an executive dashboard to consistently prompt the management team (my wife and me) before we default into summer laziness.

Knowing my patterns, I'll need a way to keep refreshing the system, otherwise the stickies will start getting stale, and the whole thing will collapse fast.  Each Friday or Saturday, we all sit down at a family dinner, and the boys inevitably scatter leaving my wife and me gazing into each others' eyes.  Perfect time to go over the current homework list!

With this atomic habit - talk to Wife about homework right after the family dinner - I'll keep the homework system fresh.

My next plan, to keep things interesting, is to set up some kind of a sync that catches assignments emailed by my boys' teachers, and adds them to my Trello board automatically.  I'll probably use my [Dispatch](https://distractedfortune.com/how-i-used-ai-to-make-technology-disappear-and-saved-my-son-from-screen-withdrawal/) system to help with that.

Finally, I need to put together the actual summer homework.  If I have to manually hunt down math problems and language texts every day, the system will die by mid-June.  The trick is to hook some new [Screamsheets](https://distractedfortune.com/how-the-desert-helped-me-solve-a-technical-problem/) into the Trello sync.  A set of daily math problems on one sheet, and a bunch of MLB news, in _Montreal French_, on another.

Technology, rendered invisible to my boys, to keep their eyes perpetually off the screens and on their work.  At least until noon.

Thanks for reading, now get back to work.

Peter
