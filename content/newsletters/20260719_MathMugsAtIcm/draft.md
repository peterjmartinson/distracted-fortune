Dear Reader,

I know you've been expecting updates from me, but honestly it's been a little hard to write these past few weeks.  Just a lot going on at home; rather, not enough going on at home!

Lest any of you dear readers think you're reading from someone who has it all together, please curb your expectations.  You're watching a work in progress, and we don't know how this work will end yet.

But I'm going to try an experiment this week.  At least, I'm planning an experiment.

You remember those [Math Mugs](https://distractedfortune.com/from-selling-drugs-to-selling-mugs/) I designed long ago?  Of course you do - you are probably drinking your coffee out of one right now!

After selling a handful to my father in law and an old math friend, there's been not much more action on my store, except for some overactive bots sniffing around the [Pythagorean Theorem](https://distractedfortune.printful.me/product/pythagorean-mug-with-proof) mug.  I put one more mug up, for Bayes's Theorem, but that's about it.

But all the mathematicians are coming to Philadelphia this next week.  It's the [International Congress of Mathematicians](https://www.mathunion.org/icm/icm-2026), the annual meeting of any math nerd who is lucky enough to still be proving theorems in the age of AI.

And, all mathematicians drink coffee.

So, I'll go out there and hand out a bunch of hot palm card advertisements for my mugs, and these consumers will shell out their hard earned college bucks for some sweet new wares.

Besides, I've started digging back into math again anyhow.  Instead of just hounding my kids to work on math over the summer, my brilliant wife convinced me that it were better to create a culture of learning.  So, I'm digging back into Gauss's covert work on elliptical functions.

And wouldn't it be nice to own a mug with a graphic of the [lemniscate sine](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/63/The_lemniscate_sine_and_cosine_related_to_the_arclength_of_the_lemniscate_of_Bernoulli_2.svg), [its integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemniscate_elliptic_functions), and a [mug of Gauss](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9b/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss.jpg) grinning up at you?

Thanks for reading, now get back to work.

Peter
